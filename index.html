About Me

My name is Yutaro Kimata, and I am a Ph.D. student in Economics at Nagoya City University in Japan. My research focuses on international trade and its implications for economic welfare, with particular interest in how trade agreements, tariff policies, and technological change influence outcomes across countries.

Research Focus

  • Causal Inference: I analyze how trade policy interventions, such as the formation of regional trade agreements or the imposition of tariffs, causally affect international trade flows. My work employs Difference-in-Differences and related econometric approaches to provide credible identification of policy impacts.
  • Structural Gravity: I structurally estimate trade elasticities to uncover how sensitive trade flows are to changes in trade costs. This research provides the empirical foundation for counterfactual policy analysis and connects observed trade patterns with theory-based parameters.
  • New Quantitative Trade: I develop and apply multi-country, multi-sector Ricardian general equilibrium models to quantify the welfare and production effects of policy shocks. In particular, I investigate how emerging technologies such as AI reshape comparative advantage and global welfare through international trade.
